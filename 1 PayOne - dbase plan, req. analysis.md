\## DATABASE PLANNING AND REQUIREMENTS ANALYSIS: PAOYNE



\# 1.0 BACKGROUND, CONTEXT AND COMPANY PROFILE



PayOne is a Ghanaian fintech and digital commerce company that commenced operations in 2020. It key  services are the following:

* Mobile money and bank payment processing.
* Merchant payment collection and settlement.
* Bulk SMS and customer notifications.
* Online shopping and merchant services.
* Utility bill payments and airtime purchases.
* Customer and merchant account management.
* Payment APIs for third-party businesses.



\# 1.1 PROJECT DEFNITION AND PROBLEM STATEMENT



PayOne commenced operations in 2020 with fewer than 100 business and individual clients. Since then, the company has experienced significant growth, expanding its customer base to over 500,000 business and individual clients.



However, the company's information management infrastructure has not evolved in line with its growth. Currently, business, customer, operational and transaction records are maintained across multiple, disconnected platforms and formats, including the company's ERP system, Excel spreadsheets, manual records and WhatsApp chats. These fragmented records adversely affect data consistency, accessibility, accuracy, security, retrieval and effective information management. The existing arrangement does not  support efficient business processes, seamless transaction processing and timely service delivery. It limits the company's ability to access reliable, real-time information, monitor transactions, coordinate business activities and generate timely management reports.



Consequently, PayOne intends to undertake a comprehensive automation and digital transformation initiative, with the development of an integrated database system as a critical component. The proposed database will provide a centralized, secure and reliable information infrastructure that supports real-time transactions, efficient service delivery, streamlined business operations, effective data management and informed managerial decision-making.

iii. Guiding principles

The underlying principles guiding the database planning and requirements stage are the following:

* Business-first design: Database design is centered around business processes and objectives, not around preferred database technologies.
* User-centred requirements: Requirements must reflect actual operational and managerial needs.
* Data integrity: Data elements must have a clear meaning, ownership and validation rule.
* Single source of truth: The database serves as an authoritative sources for critical business information.
* Data independence: Separate business requirements from implementation-specific decisions.
* Security by design: Incorporate access control, authentication, authorisation and audit requirements from the beginning.
* Scalability: Database is designed in anticipation of increasing transactions, customers, users and data
* Consistency and standardisation: Establish prior naming conventions, data definitions, formats and documentation standards.
* Traceability: Database requirements are traceable to a business objective or stakeholder need.
* Iterative validation: Maintain continuous requirements validation with stakeholders instead of only at  implementation.
